## Open issues

Problems found on the live site or in our copy of its content. Tell the site owner before adding a new one.

1. **Live consent banner links to a 404.** The banner's "Privacy Statement (Opens in a new window)" link goes to https://www.dundeecity.gov.uk/privacy-policy, which returns "Page not found" on the council's site. (Separately, https://digitaldundee.com/privacy-policy is an empty page; the real Digital Dundee statement is at https://digitaldundee.com/privacy.) Fixed in this build: our banner and footer link to `/privacy-policy`, which now holds the real Digital Dundee statement.
2. **Covid-era content.** The Enjoy Dundee page (live and here) still says non-essential travel is discouraged and that staycations are "beginning to beckon". It needs reviewing and updating.
3. **Out-of-date Challenge Fund information.** The Tay5G landing page says the funding competition is "open now". Challenge Fund 1 says it is closed. Challenge Fund 2 says "Opportunity status: Open" with an extended closing date of 13th May 2024. All three need updating or archiving, and the page's past webinar dates (December and January) are long gone.
4. **Old privacy statement.** The live privacy statement (version 12 May 2021, now at `/privacy-policy`) refers to the old website's cookie control tool, not the consent banner used here, and is more than five years old. It needs a review by Digital Dundee.
5. **Two job figures for the same statistic.** The Be Dundee page (live and here) lists "1,569 Jobs in digital tech (2017)" and "2,660 Digital tech jobs (2017)" in the same list, and they do not match. The correct figure needs confirming against the source report. For reference, £181m turnover divided by 1,569 jobs is £115k per employee, which matches the "£115k Digital tech turnover by employee" line; 2,660 would give about £68k. The whole list is also from 2017, so the statistics are out of date.
6. **Inconsistent image sizes and shapes.** Content images range from tiny to huge and from very wide to portrait. Of the 344 images in our copy of the site (excluding page headers), 135 are under 400px wide, 88 are wide logos or banners (more than 2.6 times wider than tall, 73 of them company logos), 11 are portrait, and widths run from 54px to 5083px. Event and news card images are mostly 2:1 (480x240) and 3:2 (480x320), with many squares. The live site forces every card image into a 360x200 box with `object-fit: fill`, so wide banners are squashed and square images are stretched. The worst case is the Tayside Tech Fest event: the original upload is 343x52, which the live site upscales to 1979x300 and then squeezes into the card. Our cards now pick cropping or showing the whole image from each image's shape and size (see CHANGES.md), but the source images need replacing with consistent, larger versions.
7. **Meta descriptions are often too long and inadequate.** Not to be addressed at this stage. Across the 338 descriptions in the content files, 67 are over 160 characters (63 news items; the longest is 548), 110 end in "..." where they were cut off mid-sentence (87 events, 10 resources, all 13 success stories), 37 are under 50 characters or nearly empty (28 of them resources) and 40 are identical to another entry. Most are just the opening of the article body, so they do not summarise it or give a reason to click. The 28 hand-written page descriptions are all within 160 characters, though some are generic ("Enjoy Dundee, Tay Tech and Tay5G - a portal for...").
